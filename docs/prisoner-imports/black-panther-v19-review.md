(<https://www.marxists.org/history/usa/pubs/black-panther/index.htm>), issues no. 2, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 11 (122 pages total). Each named
individual below was presented by the paper as a political prisoner or the subject of a
political prosecution. Descriptions record what the paper reported (1979), with a few
well-established later outcomes noted.

## Named but NOT added (with reason)

- **Martin Luther King, Jr.** and **Marcus Garvey** — appeared as Black History Month historical features (MLK's 'Letter From Birmingham Jail' reprint; Garvey essay). Iconic; almost certainly already in the database.
- **George Jackson** — included above as a Black Panther Party figure; if already present, prisoner:add will skip him.
- **Sacco and Vanzetti** — invoked by Huey Newton only as a historical rhetorical parallel.
- **John Spenkelink**, **Willie Jasper Darden** — death-penalty cases the paper covered as anti-death-penalty causes, but ordinary murder convictions rather than political-prisoner cases.
- **Samuel L. Moore, Jr.** — died after guard beatings, but imprisoned for an ordinary robbery; a prisoner-abuse case, not a political prosecution.
- **Melvin Johnson** — Terrence Johnson's brother, a 6-month sentence for an alleged $29 theft (minor, not political).
- **Charles Diggs** — U.S. Representative; a payroll-fraud conviction the paper called trumped-up, but not a political-prisoner case.
- **Mujahid Halim (Talmadge Hayer)** — the confessed and rightly-convicted killer of Malcolm X (he asserted his co-defendants' innocence); not a wrongful/political case.
- **'Harold'** (Atmore) — only a first name was legible in the scan; cannot be added responsibly.
