# Political prisoners found in The Black Panther, Vol. 17 (1977)

Read page-by-page from the marxists.org archive (<https://www.marxists.org/history/usa/pubs/black-panther/index.htm>), all 28 Vol. 17 issues (~780 pages).
Descriptions record what the paper reported in 1977, with a few well-established later outcomes noted
(e.g., the Rubin Carter and Malcolm X-defendant exonerations, Assata Shakur's asylum in Cuba).

## Additional international mentions (catalogued, not scripted)

Vol. 17 covered anti-apartheid and anti-colonial repression heavily. Beyond the three above, named cases included:
- **South Africa:** Percy Qoboza, Nelson & Winnie Mandela, Fatima Meer, Robert Sobukwe, Mapetla Mohapi, Nthato Motlana, Peter Jones, and a large photo-roster of detained/banned figures (Ellen Khuzwayo, Aubrey Mokoena, Thenjiwe Mtintso, Kenneth Rachidi, and others).
- **Zimbabwe (Rhodesia):** ANC(Z)/ZAPU nationalists sentenced to death and secretly hanged under the Law and Order (Maintenance) Act — Robert Bhebe, Painos Zehama, Isaac Mabika, Jameson Kasili, Rabson Mushonja, Talphanos Moyo, and others; Filemon Nangolo (Namibia).
- **Philippines:** New People's Army/opposition figures sentenced to death — Bernabe Buscayno, Victor Corpus, Benigno Aquino.
- **West Germany:** the Baader-Meinhof prisoners who died at Stammheim (Andreas Baader, Gudrun Ensslin, Jan-Carl Raspe).
- **Chile:** MIR figures disappeared under Pinochet.

## Named but NOT added (with reason)

- **James Earl Ray**, **Sirhan Sirhan** — the King/RFK assassins; covered via conspiracy theories, not political-prisoner cases.
- **Erskine Burrows & Larry Tacklyn** (Bermuda Black Beret Cadres) — hanged for assassinating the British governor, a police commissioner and others; political violence resulting in deaths, not a sympathetic wrongful-imprisonment case.
- **Hamaas Abdul Khaalis / Abdul Muzikir** (1977 Hanafi Siege) — an armed hostage takeover that killed a reporter.
- **Buddy Cochran** — drove a car through a Klan rally injuring 32 (too indiscriminate).
- **Mark Rudd** (Weathermen) — surrendered to misdemeanor charges, released on his own recognizance (no imprisonment).
- **Marcus Garvey** — historical Black-History references, already represented in the database.
- The ECCO/Hancock County fraud defendants, and numerous letter-writers who described themselves as political prisoners without a case being stated (Harry James Snow, Rufus Averhart/Zola Azania, John Payne, and others).
