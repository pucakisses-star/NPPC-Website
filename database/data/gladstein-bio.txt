Richard Gladstein was a San Francisco labor and civil-liberties attorney and longtime counsel for the International Longshore and Warehouse Union. He represented Harry Bridges in the federal government's repeated attempts to deport the union leader and defended Communist Party leaders Gus Hall and Robert G. Thompson during the landmark 1949 Foley Square Smith Act trial. At the conclusion of the trial, Judge Harold Medina ordered Gladstein and the other defense lawyers be jailed for criminal contempt for accusing the judge of having been "biased and prejudiced" during the trial. Gladstein was then kept in restraints for seven weeks while being transported from New York to the Federal Correctional Institution at Texarkana, Texas and eventually released early after 5 months out of his 6 month sentence.
