## Rights: this one is not free, and the record should say so

Every other portrait in this archive is public domain, no-known-copyright
or explicitly fair-use-argued. This is neither.

**Licence type: rights-managed. Not released.** The distributor licenses
it commercially and there is a named, identifiable photographer. NPPC
does not hold a licence.

The case for using it anyway is the ordinary one for a non-profit
historical archive: a fifty-six-year-old news photograph of a deceased
public figure, reproduced at thumbnail resolution, illustrating the exact
event the biography describes, on a page that neither sells the image nor
competes with the market for it. That is a reasonable fair-use posture.
It is not a licence, and it is not a court.

If NPPC would rather not carry the exposure, there is no free substitute
known: Jacobs spent twenty-seven years avoiding being photographed, and
the surviving images of him are nearly all press photographs from these
four days in 1969. The honest options are to license this frame, or to
run the profile without a portrait.

## Why this is him

The identification does not rest on the filename or on the curator's say-so:

1. The distributor's own caption names him and describes the football
   helmet visible in the frame.
2. The stone plinth behind him is the base of the Haymarket police
   statue, which the Weathermen bombed on October 8, 1969 — the caption
   calls it "the defaced base of a statue," and the partial lettering is
   visible in the crop.
3. The date, October 11, 1969, is the day of the final march, and matches
   the contemporary eyewitness account already cited in his biography:
   that he was still at liberty and addressing the crowd immediately
   before that march. The photograph and the arrest record agree.
