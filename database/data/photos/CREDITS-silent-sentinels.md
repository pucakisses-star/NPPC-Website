# Silent Sentinel portraits — provenance and rights

Six portraits attached by `database/data/attach-silent-sentinel-photos.sh`
(batch 31). All are public domain or carry no known copyright
restrictions. Every one was verified against its archival caption before
use — the caption text, not just the filename, is what confirms the
sitter.

All are cropped to 525×700 to match the existing portrait convention.
The originals are unmodified apart from trimming archival mounts and
cropping; no retouching.

---

## julia-emory.jpg — Julia Ridgely Emory

- **Source:** Library of Congress, Records of the National Woman's Party,
  digital id `mnwp.274011` — <https://hdl.loc.gov/loc.mss/mnwp.274011>
- **Obtained via:** Wikimedia Commons, *Miss Julia Emory 274011v.jpg*
  (2352×3241), because loc.gov serves a Cloudflare challenge to
  non-interactive clients.
- **Rights:** Public domain.
- **Archival caption:** "Formal portrait, head and shoulders, Julia
  Emory, left profile, wearing v-neck dress or blouse with ruffled
  collar. Julia Emory of Baltimore, Md., daughter of state senator
  D. H. Emory…"
- **Why this is her:** the caption names Baltimore and her father the
  state senator, matching the Maryland Silent Sentinel in this record.
- **Processing:** white archival mount trimmed, then cropped.

## agnes-chase.jpg — Mary Agnes Chase

- **Source:** Smithsonian Institution Archives.
- **Obtained via:** Wikimedia Commons, *Mary Agnes Chase (1869-1963),
  sitting at desk with specimens.jpg* (3016×2461), because
  siarchives.si.edu returns 403 to non-interactive clients.
- **Rights:** No known copyright restrictions.
- **Archival caption:** "Mary Agnes Chase (1869-1963) specialized in the
  study of grasses and conducted extensive field work in South America,
  often personally funding her research trips…"
- **Why this is her:** the caption carries her life dates, which match
  this record exactly (April 20, 1869 – September 24, 1963).
- **Processing:** landscape original cropped to portrait around the
  sitter; she is at her desk with pressed grass specimens.

## anna-herkner.jpg — Anna Herkner

- **Source:** Maryland Women's Heritage Center, suffrage biographies —
  <https://mdwomensheritagecenter.org/directory-suffragebios/listing/anna-herkner-1879-1959/>
- **Rights:** Historical newspaper halftone, published c. 1919; public
  domain by age.
- **Why this is her:** THE IMAGE ITSELF IS CAPTIONED "MISS ANNA HERKNER"
  in the printed original. That is independent typographic confirmation
  of the spelling, and it is the reason this record was renamed from
  "Anne Herkimer" in batch 30 — Doris Stevens's appendix misprinted the
  surname.
- **Quality note:** the source is a 216×343 newsprint halftone, the only
  image located. It is soft when enlarged and the dot screen is visible.
  It is kept because it is authentic, captioned, and the alternative is
  no portrait at all. Replace it if a photographic original surfaces.
- **Processing:** cropped; the printed decorative border is part of the
  original and is left in place.

## bertha-moller.jpg — Bertha Berglin Moller

- **Source:** Hennepin County Library, via the Digital Public Library of
  America.
- **Obtained via:** Wikimedia Commons, *Bertha Moller - DPLA -
  58a5672e7129b88be03b48954a06f697.jpg* (2292×3556).
- **Rights:** Public domain.
- **Archival caption:** "Moller is a student in the University of
  Minnesota law school and an officer in the National Woman's Party."
- **Why this is her, and not the other one:** the Library of Congress
  also holds a portrait captioned "Mrs. Bertha C. Moller"
  (`mnwp.155001`). That caption gives a middle initial C and no
  biographical detail, so it cannot be tied to Bertha *Berglin* Moller.
  The Hennepin image names both Minnesota and the National Woman's
  Party, which matches this record on two independent points, so it was
  chosen instead. The LOC image is deliberately NOT used.

## berthe-arnold.jpg — Berthe Louise Arnold

- **Source:** Library of Congress, Records of the National Woman's Party,
  digital id `mnwp.147007` — <https://hdl.loc.gov/loc.mss/mnwp.147007>
- **Obtained via:** Wikimedia Commons, *Berthe Arnold 147007v
  (cropped).jpg* (1898×2751).
- **Rights:** Public domain.
- **Archival caption:** "Informal, full-length portrait of Berthe Arnold
  of Colorado Springs, Colorado, wearing a hat and fur-trimmed coat with
  a bouquet of flowers pinned on the front, looking downward at an urn
  containing a burning 'watchfire' maintained in front…"
- **Why this is her:** the caption names Colorado Springs, matching this
  record, and this is the exact LOC resource cited in the research
  dossier.
- **Note on the image:** she is looking down into the burning watchfire,
  so her face is turned away. It is kept as the identified image of her
  at the January 1919 demonstrations she was arrested at, rather than
  substituting an unidentified face-forward portrait.

# Unrelated addition (batch 38)

## otis-w-smith.jpg — Dr. Otis Wesley Smith

- **Source:** Digital Library of Georgia, Atlanta Funeral Programs
  Collection — his own 2007 funeral program,
  `dlg.galileo.usg.edu/data/aarl/afpc/pdfs/aarl_afpc_smithotiswesley.pdf`
- **Why this is him:** it is his funeral program. The cover reads
  "The Homegoing Celebration of Dr. Otis Wesley Smith — Sunrise May 12,
  1925 – Sunset February 5, 2007", so the portrait and the vital dates in
  this record authenticate each other from a single document. It is a
  family publication, which is a far stronger identification than an
  uncaptioned web image.
- **Processing:** page 1 of the PDF extracted at its embedded resolution
  (3556×4543), then DESKEWED. The page was scanned 0.507° off level,
  measured by fitting a line to the top edge of the printed photograph
  across 114 sample columns, and rotated back by that amount. The
  photograph's rectangle was then re-detected on the levelled image
  (1646×1919) and cropped to 525×700 at full height, so nothing is lost
  from the top of his head and no page margin shows at the bottom. No
  retouching.

## samuel-green.jpg — Reverend Samuel Green

- **This is not a photograph.** It is a wood engraving published in
  William Still's *The Underground Rail Road* (1872). Still noted that
  many of his engravings were produced from "photographs from life", and
  Green appears to have sat for a portrait in Philadelphia shortly after
  his 1862 release — but **the underlying photograph has not been
  located**, so the engraving is one remove from the sitter.
- **Status:** the highest-confidence authenticated likeness available. It
  is kept on that basis, with the caveat recorded here rather than left
  implicit, because a nineteenth-century engraving reads as a photograph
  at thumbnail size and is not one.
- **Rights:** published 1872; public domain.
