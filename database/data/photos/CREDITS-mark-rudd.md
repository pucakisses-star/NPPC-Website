## Why this is him

The publisher's own markup names him: the `alt` attribute is
`Photo Mark Rudd` and the `<figcaption>` is `Mark Rudd`. The caption sits
inside Lopate's account of the 1968 Columbia strike, a few lines after
Tom Hayden's description of "the chairman of Columbia SDS, Mark Rudd".
The identification is the publisher's, not an inference from a filename.

## Rights

Uncredited, and not released under any free licence. Columbia Magazine
publishes it without naming a photographer, so the rights holder is
unidentified — most likely the university or a student photographer from
the *Columbia Daily Spectator* era, but that is a guess and is not
recorded as fact here.

The use is the ordinary one for a non-profit historical archive: a
fifty-eight-year-old photograph of a public figure, reproduced small,
illustrating the biography of the person depicted, on a page that does
not sell it. That is a reasonable fair-use posture rather than a licence.
It is a weaker claim than a public-domain image and a stronger position
than the Jacobs portrait in batch 166, which is actively licensed by a
stock agency; this one has no known commercial market being displaced.

If Columbia can identify the photographer, a credit line should be added
to this file and the image kept or dropped on their answer.
