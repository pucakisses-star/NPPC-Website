# Audit — imprisonments with no apparent political motivation

Read-only audit of all 8,570 records. **No data changed.** Everything below is a
recommendation.

This is the audit the scope audit said could not be done by pattern. It can't be
done by pattern *alone*, so this used patterns to build a shortlist and then read
every candidate.

## Why the obvious method fails

A keyword sweep for ordinary-crime vocabulary returns 57 records, and **almost
all of them are among the most clearly political in the archive** — because in a
political frame-up the charge *is* an ordinary crime. The matches included:

- **sixteen ILD and Civil Rights Congress "legal lynching" cases** — Black men
  framed on rape charges (`raleigh-speller`, `jess-hollins`, `ocie-jugger`,
  `jeremiah-reeves`, `clyde-brown`, `walter-brown` and ten more). The word "rape"
  is what flagged them. They are the archive's core.
- **`sam-jordan`, `willie-thomas`, `jesse-crawford`** — flagged by "gang",
  matching **chain gang**.
- **`joshua-schulte` and `keith-gartenlaub`** — flagged by "child porn", which in
  both cases was an add-on charge found during an espionage investigation. That
  pattern is itself the political fact.

Filtering instead for records with no ideology, no affiliation and no political
vocabulary anywhere narrows 8,570 to 30. Reading those 30, plus the 57, gives the
findings below.

## Tier 1 — no political motivation stated anywhere in the record

Five records. In each, nothing in the description, charges, ideology or
affiliation fields connects the imprisonment to politics.

### `larry-hoover`
Founder of the Gangster Disciples; imprisoned since 1973 on an Illinois murder
conviction (150–200 years) plus a 1997 federal life sentence for running a
continuing criminal enterprise. The only political element in the record is that
**Trump commuted the federal sentence in 2025** — an act of politics by someone
else, not a political motivation for the imprisonment. There is a case to be made
about the Gangster Disciples' political turn in the 1990s (21st Century VOTE);
the record makes none of it. The strongest candidate here.

### `ron-glick`
Felony sexual assault involving a 13-year-old, Montana, convicted 2005, 20 years
with 15 suspended. The record's only argument is that he maintains innocence and
keeps litigating. **An innocence claim is not a political motivation**, and no
political context of any kind is asserted.

### `martin-s-kimber`
Planted two pounds of mercury around an Albany hospital, including on food served
to patients. Pleaded guilty to chemical-weapon and product-tampering charges, 168
months. **No motive of any kind is stated** — political or otherwise.

### `kyle-shaw`
Bombed a Starbucks on the Upper East Side in 2009 at nineteen, and was planning a
second when arrested. No one hurt; 42 months. **No motive is stated.** A bombing
of a corporate target could be political, and if it was, the record needs to say
so; as written it is a bombing with no politics attached.

### `sanyika-shakur`
The record's own account: his incarcerations were for "serious violent and
weapons-related offenses", and the activism it describes is anti-gang messaging
that came *after* and was shaped by them. **He was not imprisoned for the
advocacy** — the record says so itself.

## Tier 2 — the record is missing the politics, rather than lacking it

Two records that read as non-political only because a fact is absent. These need
research, not removal.

### `sonia-anayibe-rojas`
Described only as "a Colombian drug trafficker" whose trial was unfair. But
Anayibe Rojas Valderrama, alias **"Sonia"**, is on the public record as a FARC
commander — the finance chief of the FARC's Southern Bloc — captured in 2004 and
extradited to the United States. If that is the same person, this is a captured
combatant of an armed political movement and the record is missing its entire
context. **Verify the identification before doing anything else.** Note the
archive already holds several FARC extraditions (`jorge-rodriguez-mendieta`,
`hugo-carvajal`).

### `barry-cooper`
Flagged by the sweep for narcotics vocabulary, but reading it he is
**unambiguously political**: a former narcotics officer turned drug-war critic,
prosecuted for filing a false police report after a sting that proved his police
department had lied in search-warrant affidavits — which overturned a woman's
conviction after four years. He ran for Texas Attorney General on police reform,
fled to Brazil, and **was granted political asylum**.

Two data problems follow from that, both fixable: his ideology and affiliation
fields are empty, and **his exile flags are unset** despite the record saying he
lives in exile with asylum. He would not have appeared in this audit at all if
those fields were populated.

## Tier 3 — two category questions, not individual errors

These are coherent groups where the archive has implicitly taken a position. They
want a policy decision, not case-by-case triage.

### Threats against elected officials — at least 8 records

`eric-hafner` (20 years — threats to kill New Jersey officials, police, judges
and their families), `carlos-bayon`, `christopher-kent-podlesnik`,
`eugene-huelsman`, `matthew-lee-comiskey`, `rickey-johnson`, `suzanne-muscara`
(fake anthrax to Senator Collins), `deep-alpesh-kumar-patel`.

Every one is speech aimed at a political target, and every one is a threat of
violence against a named person. If the archive's line is that political
motivation is enough, they all belong; if it is that the act must not be a threat
of violence, none of them do. **It cannot be both**, and at present these sit
alongside people jailed for picketing.

### Property and weapons offences during the 2020 protests — at least 5 records

`channel-lewis` (lookout in a CVS pharmacy burglary during the Louisville
unrest), `shawn-collins` (attempted ATM theft, Philadelphia), `kyle-olson`
(felon-in-possession, Madison), `jesse-clark` (arson of the Nashville
courthouse), `mena-yousif` (accessory — helped her husband flee after an arson).

Same question in a different decade: does being arrested *during* a protest make
the prosecution political, when the act charged is burglary or gun possession?
`jesse-clark`'s courthouse arson has an obvious political reading;
`shawn-collins`'s ATM does not. They are currently treated identically.

## Contested — worth a second opinion, not a recommendation

**`jorge-p-cornell`** — Latin Kings leader in Greensboro, 28 years on RICO. He
publicly campaigned for peace between street groups and against Greensboro police
gang-policing; prosecutors said that was cover for racketeering. The record
states both fairly. Genuinely contested, and the kind of case the archive exists
to hold open rather than settle.

**`timothy-reed`** — his underlying conviction was armed robbery, which is not
political, but the imprisonment the record is actually about is a parole
revocation his supporters argued was **retaliation for his prisoner-rights
advocacy**. That is political. Keep.

**`samuel-r-caldwell`** — the first person convicted under the Marihuana Tax Act,
1937. A prohibition landmark rather than a political prosecution, but a defensible
inclusion if the archive covers drug-war test cases.

## Limits

This found records that are *silent* about political motivation. It cannot find a
record that asserts a political motive the sources do not support — that would
need checking each claim against outside evidence, one at a time. Nor did it
examine the 7,436 records carrying an ideology or affiliation tag, on the
assumption that a curator applied those deliberately; if tags were applied in
bulk during import, that assumption is worth testing separately.

The five Tier 1 records are the ones I would stand behind. Everything below that
is a question rather than a finding.

## Recommendations

1. **Decide the two category questions first.** They cover thirteen records
   between them and settle more than case-by-case review would.
2. **Verify `sonia-anayibe-rojas` before touching her record** — she may be a
   FARC commander with the context missing.
3. **Fix `barry-cooper` regardless of anything else**: populate his ideology and
   affiliation, and set his exile flags. He is in exile with political asylum and
   the record does not say so in any queryable field.
4. **Tier 1 is yours to decide.** As with the scope audit, `under_review = true`
   hides a record from public queries without deleting it, if you want them off
   the site while you think.
5. The five Tier 1 records are also all missing sentences, dates, or both —
   `ron-glick`, `sanyika-shakur` and `sonia-anayibe-rojas` publish a zero counter.
   Whatever is decided about inclusion, none of them is currently a usable record.
